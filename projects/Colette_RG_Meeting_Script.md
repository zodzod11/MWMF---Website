# Colette + RG Meeting: Talking Points & Script

**Tomorrow — Review of Concept Narrative, Scope of Services & Next Steps**

---

## Her Position Going In

| Fact | Leverage |
|---|---|
| She has **zero startup capital** | Cannot sign a contract she can't pay |
| She has a PhD, research, and clinical expertise | RG needs her credibility to build the org |
| Cummings LOI deadline is **Sept 16** (9 days) | Urgency is real — but she can write the LOI herself |
| The concept narrative is a **draft** | She controls the final vision — not RG |
| The 3% success fee is **ethically problematic** for a nonprofit | AFP discourages it, funders may question it |

---

## Her Objectives (In Order)

1. **Be honest about money** — she can't pay the retainer. Don't sign something she can't honor.
2. **Protect the Cummings timeline** — LOI must be submitted by Sept 16 regardless of whether RG is involved.
3. **Keep the relationship warm** — she wants to work with RG *later* when she has funding.
4. **Correct the concept narrative** — make sure programs match *her* vision, not RG's assumptions.
5. **Get free advice** — use the meeting to get Paris's input on the LOI, board structure, and fiscal sponsorship without paying for it.

---

## Script

### Opening (Set the tone)

> *"Paris, thank you for putting these materials together. I can see you've put real thought into this. I want to be straightforward with you about where I am, because I respect your time and I want to find a way to work together that actually works."*

**Why this works:** You're appreciative, honest, and direct. You're not wasting his time.

---

### Point 1 — The Money Conversation (Most Important)

> *"I'm going to be honest with you. I don't have startup capital right now. I can't sign a $4,500 retainer — or any retainer — out of my personal budget. I don't want to sign something I can't pay. That wouldn't be fair to either of us."*

**Pause. Let him respond.**

**What he might say:**

| Pushback | Your response |
|---|---|
| "We can work out a payment plan" | *"I appreciate that, but I genuinely don't have the cash flow right now — not even for installments. I'm a professor. My salary is fixed. I can't commit to payments I'm not sure I can make."* |
| "This is an investment in your vision" | *"I agree it's an investment. And I want to make it. But I need to be responsible about what I can actually afford. I'd rather be honest now than have a problem later."* |
| "What if we do a higher success fee instead?" | *"I've been advised that a percentage-based fee structure is problematic for a nonprofit. The Association of Fundraising Professionals discourages it, and some funders may question it. I want to keep the organization clean from the start."* |

---

### Point 2 — What She CAN Do (The Pivot)

After you've been honest about the money, pivot to what you CAN do:

> *"Here's what I can do. I'm going to handle the incorporation and the Cummings LOI myself this week. I've already started drafting the essays — this is my research, my story, my vision. I can write it. I can file the paperwork. And I can meet the Sept 16 deadline on my own."*

**Then immediately offer a path forward for RG:**

> *"But I don't want this to be the end of our conversation. I want to work with you — when the timing is right. Here's what I'd propose: I do the startup work myself this month. I incorporate, I submit the LOI, I get the basic structure in place. Then, once there's some funding or even a solid prospect, I come back to you for the things I actually need help with — grant writing, board development, strategic planning. We start smaller, on a project basis, when there's money to pay for it."*

**Why this works:** You're not rejecting him. You're deferring and re-scoping. You're showing you're resourceful and responsible. You're giving him a clear path to get paid later.

---

### Point 3 — The 3% Success Fee (If He Brings It Up)

If Paris tries to push for a percentage-based arrangement:

> *"I've thought about this carefully. A percentage-based fee creates complications for a nonprofit. The Association of Fundraising Professionals advises against it. Some funders ask about it in their due diligence. For a brand-new organization that's already going to be scrutinized, I don't want to give anyone a reason to question our financial practices. I'd rather pay a fair flat fee for specific work."*

**If he pushes back:**

> *"I'm not trying to shortchange you. I want you to be compensated fairly. But I need to protect the organization's reputation from day one. If we work together, I'd prefer a flat fee or a per-project arrangement. That's clean, transparent, and defensible."*

---

### Point 4 — The Concept Narrative (Correcting the Vision)

> *"I reviewed the concept narrative. I appreciate the work you put into it. I want to share a few thoughts on how to make it more aligned with my vision."*

**Specific corrections to raise:**

| RG's draft says | What Colette should say |
|---|---|
| 4 program areas (including Economic & Family Stability) | *"My focus is on three core programs: Support Circles, Family Education, and Wellness Navigation. Economic stability is important, but it's not a core program — it's something we connect families to through partners. I want to keep the scope tight."* |
| Doesn't use the CONNECT → NAVIGATE → RECONNECT model | *"The disconnection-to-reconnection framework is the heart of my research. I want that language front and center — it's what makes this organization different."* |
| Broad language about "underserved communities" | *"The primary population is Black mothers, Haitian women, and immigrant women in Greater Boston. I want that specificity in the narrative. It's not a general maternal health org."* |

---

### Point 5 — The Cummings Question (Get His Free Advice)

Even though she's not paying him, she can still pick his brain:

> *"One question I'd love your input on: the Cummings Foundation doesn't generally fund 'relatively new entities.' I'm considering fiscal sponsorship as an alternative. Do you have any thoughts on that? Or any organizations you think would be a good fiscal sponsor fit?"*

**Why this works:** You're asking for his expertise without committing to pay for it. If he gives good advice, great. If he's unwilling to help without a contract, that tells you something.

---

### Closing

> *"I want to be clear: I'm not walking away from working with you. I'm walking toward a model that actually works for both of us. I do the startup work myself. I get the organization off the ground. And when there's funding, you're the first person I call for the work I actually need help with. Does that feel fair to you?"*

---

## Cheat Sheet (Key Phrases)

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| He asks for payment she can't make | *"I don't want to sign something I can't honor. That wouldn't be fair to either of us."* |
| He pushes the 3% fee | *"For a nonprofit, percentage-based fees create complications with funders. I want to keep the organization clean."* |
| He seems disappointed she's not signing | *"I'm not saying no to working together. I'm saying let's find a model that works when there's actually money to pay for it."* |
| He offers to reduce the retainer | *"I appreciate that, but right now any amount is a risk I can't take. I need to be responsible."* |
| He asks what she needs from him | *"Your input on the Cummings strategy and fiscal sponsorship would be valuable. Beyond that, let's check back in 60 days when I have a foundation in place."* |

## Bottom Line

| Paris responds well | Paris pushes back |
|---|---|
| She's honest, he respects her. She keeps the relationship warm. She does the work herself. She calls him when she has funding. | If he's unwilling to be flexible on payment or structure, then he's not the right partner for a startup nonprofit with no capital. |

**This conversation is a test.** If Paris is genuinely interested in the mission, he'll be flexible and stay engaged. If he's only interested in the fee, he'll push for a contract regardless of her situation. His response will tell her everything.
