# Part 1: Banking

## Primary Recommendation — Eastern Bank Charitable Checking

### Why Eastern Bank

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Fees** | $0 monthly / $0 minimum — free checking for 501(c)(3)s |
| **Opening requirement** | 501(c)(3) determination letter + Articles + EIN + Board resolution |
| **Branch network** | Strong in Dorchester, Roxbury, Hyde Park, JP — exactly the communities you serve |
| **Decision-making** | Local branch managers have real authority. Relationship matters. |
| **Nonprofit lending** | Formal Nonprofit Banking group. Grant bridge loans available as you grow. |
| **Online banking** | Free online banking, mobile deposit, bill pay, QuickBooks sync |
| **International wires** | Available ($25–35 fee) |
| **CRA alignment** | Eastern is a mutual bank with deep community development commitments. Your mission (Black maternal health, Haitian immigrant support) aligns directly with their CRA priorities. |

## Backup Recommendation — DCU (Digital Federal Credit Union)

### When to use DCU

Use DCU **only if** you need an account before the 501(c)(3) letter arrives and Eastern Bank can't open one without it.

### DCU Profile

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Fees** | Low or no monthly fees. Easy to open with minimal balance. |
| **Opening requirement** | EIN + Articles — no 501(c)(3) required |
| **Best for** | Temporary holding account. Not a long-term home. |
| **Downside** | No nonprofit lending. No merchant services. Limited branch network in your communities. |

### Switching plan

If you open DCU first:

1. Open DCU account (temporary)
2. When 501(c)(3) arrives → Open Eastern Bank account
3. Transfer all funds from DCU to Eastern
4. Forward any grantors to new account details
5. Close DCU after 30 days

# Part 2: Credit Cards

## When to apply

Apply **the same week** the 501(c)(3) determination letter arrives. Having the letter lets you apply as an org without personal guarantees.

## Ranking — Full Analysis

---

### 🥇 1. Capital One Spark Classic for Business

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| **Annual fee** | **$0** |
| **Personal guarantee** | **No** — with 501(c)(3) letter, approved on org credit |
| **Rewards** | **1.5% unlimited cashback** on every purchase. No categories. No caps. |
| **Sign-up bonus** | $200–$500 (varies by promotion) |
| **Foreign transaction fee** | **0%** |
| **Online platform** | Best-in-class. Clean interface. Easy QuickBooks sync. |
| **Employee cards** | Free. Set individual limits. |
| **Accepted** | Everywhere (Visa). |

**Why ranked #1:** You are a flat-spend organization. You don't spend heavily in any bonus category (travel, shipping, advertising). A flat 1.5% cashback on everything is optimal — it rewards every dollar equally. The online experience is the best of any option. 0% foreign transaction fee is a bonus if you ever order materials or books from international sources or support diaspora connections.

**Estimated annual cashback at $10K spend: $150**
**Estimated annual cashback at $25K spend: $375**

### 🥇 2. Bank of America Nonprofit Card

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| **Annual fee** | **$0** |
| **Personal guarantee** | **No** — with 501(c)(3) letter |
| **Rewards** | 1.5% unlimited cashback |
| **Sign-up bonus** | None for nonprofit version |

**Why ranked #2:** Functionally identical to Capital One on rewards. Loses because (a) you chose Eastern Bank for checking, not BofA, so the integration advantage doesn't apply, and (b) 3% foreign transaction fee. Only pick this if you have a strong reason to dislike Capital One.

### 🥇 3. American Express Business Platinum (Nonprofit Pricing)

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| **Annual fee** | **$0** — normally $695, waived for qualifying 501(c)(3)s |
| **Personal guarantee** | **No** |
| **Rewards** | Membership Rewards points (more complex than cashback) |
| **Sign-up bonus** | Large — often 100K+ points |
| **Foreign transaction fee** | 0% |
| **Best feature** | Travel protections: trip delay insurance, cancellation coverage, lounge access, purchase protection |

**Why ranked #3:** *Only if Colette travels.* If she attends 2+ conferences per year (APHA, Black Maternal Health Week, Zero to Three, etc.), the travel benefits and sign-up bonus make this valuable. If all work is local, skip it. Also — AmEx is not accepted everywhere. Some community vendors and churches won't take it, so you'd still need a Visa/MC backup.

**Verdict:** Get this *in addition to* Capital One, not instead of it.
