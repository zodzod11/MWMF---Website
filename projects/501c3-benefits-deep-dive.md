## 1.1 Federal Tax Exemption

| Detail | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **What** | The org pays **no federal income tax** on income related to its tax-exempt purpose (grants, donations, program service revenue) |
| **IRS Code** | Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code |
| **Unrelated Business Income Tax (UBIT)** | If the org earns income from an activity unrelated to its mission (e.g., selling merchandise not tied to programming), that income *is* taxable. Threshold: $1,000+/year triggers Form 990-T. |

🔴 **Act Now:** This is the core benefit. Every grant and donation you receive is tax-free.

## 1.2 State Tax Exemptions

### Massachusetts Sales Tax Exemption

| Detail | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **What** | The org does not pay MA sales tax (6.25%) on purchases of goods and services for its own use |
| **How to get it** | Apply to **MA Department of Revenue** for a Sales Tax Exemption Certificate (Form ST-2) |
| **What it covers** | Office supplies, equipment, program materials, furniture, software |
| **What it does NOT cover** | Meals for events (unless structured correctly), items resold to the public |
| **Estimated savings** | 6.25% on every purchase. On $50K in annual spending = **~$3,125 saved**. |

🔴 **Act Now:** Apply the same week you get the 501(c)(3) letter.

| Detail | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **What** | Real property owned by a 501(c)(3) and used for exempt purposes is exempt from property tax |
| **When it matters** | Only if/when the org buys or leases a physical space that is classified as "owned by the org" |
| **Relevance now** | None — but when you move beyond Colette's home office or Wheaton College, remember this |

## 1.4 Tax-Deductible Donations

| Detail | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **What** | Donors can deduct their contributions from their federal income tax |

## 1.6 Legal Protections

### Volunteer Protection Act

| Detail | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **What** | Federal law that protects volunteers of 501(c)(3) orgs from personal liability for ordinary negligence while performing volunteer work |
| **Does NOT cover** | Gross negligence, willful misconduct, operating a vehicle, or conduct outside the scope of the volunteer role |
| **Why it matters** | Your support circle facilitators, family educators, and peer navigators can volunteer without worrying about being personally sued if something goes wrong |

🔴 **Act Now:** Include a reference to volunteer liability protection in your volunteer orientation materials.

### Limited Liability Protection for Board Members

| Detail | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **What** | Board members of MA nonprofits are generally not personally liable for the org's debts or obligations if they act in good faith and within their duties |
| **Requirement** | The org must maintain D&O insurance and the board must follow the bylaws and conflict of interest policy |
| **Why it matters** | Your board members (including Colette and yourself) can serve without personal financial risk for ordinary business decisions |

🔴 **Act Now:** Confirm D&O insurance is in place before any board member approves a spending decision.

### Charitable Immunity (Massachusetts)

| Detail | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **What** | Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 231 limits the liability of charitable corporations for tort claims |
| **Cap** | Liability is generally capped at $20,000 per claim for charitable organizations |
| **Why it matters** | If a participant at a support circle is injured, your exposure is limited |

🟡 **Year 1 Priority:** Understand this when you launch your first in-person program.

## 2.5 Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF)

This is **potentially the single most valuable personal benefit** of working at a nonprofit.

| Detail | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **What** | Federal program that forgives the remaining balance on Direct Loans after 120 qualifying payments (10 years) while working full-time for a qualifying employer |
| **Qualifying employer** | Any 501(c)(3) organization qualifies |
| **Qualifying employment** | Must be **full-time** (30+ hrs/week or the employer's definition of full-time) |
| **Loan types covered** | Direct Subsidized/Unsubsidized Loans, Direct PLUS Loans, Direct Consolidation Loans |
| **What gets forgiven** | The remaining balance after 120 payments — not just the interest. This can be tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars. |

### How it applies

| Person | Has Student Loans? | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Colette** | Has a PhD — likely has federal student loans | ✅ If she works full-time for the nonprofit, every qualifying monthly payment counts toward PSLF |
| **Zack** | Unknown — check your loan type | ✅ If you have Direct Loans and work 30+ hrs/week for the org |

### What to do

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Check if your loans are Direct Loans (not FFEL, Perkins, or private) |
| 2 | If FFEL → **consolidate** into a Direct Consolidation Loan before applying for PSLF |
| 3 | Submit the **PSLF Employment Certification Form** (ECF) annually, or when you leave the employer |
| 4 | Work 120 qualifying months (10 years, need not be consecutive) |
| 5 | Apply for forgiveness |

**⚠️ Critical:** Only full-time employment counts. Part-time at the nonprofit does not qualify. If Colette is working 8 hrs/week for the org and also teaching at Wheaton, those 8 hrs don't count toward PSLF — her *primary* employment must be with the 501(c)(3).

🟡 **Year 1 Priority:** If either of you has federal student loans, submit the Employment Certification Form as soon as the 501(c)(3) is active and you have qualifying employment. The clock starts on the date of qualifying employment, not the date the form is approved.

# Part 3: What You Need to Be Careful About

---

## 3.1 Private Inurement / Private Benefit

| Rule | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **The core restriction** | No part of a 501(c)(3)'s earnings may **inure** to the benefit of any private individual (founder, board member, officer) |
| **Translation** | You cannot use the nonprofit to enrich yourself. Everything must be reasonable, documented, and in service of the mission. |
| **Consequence of violation** | IRS can revoke 501(c)(3) status and impose intermediate sanctions (excise taxes on the individual and the org) |

### What's allowed vs not allowed

| Activity | Allowed | Not Allowed |
|---|---|---|
| **Reasonable salary for actual work** | ✅ Yes | ❌ Paying yourself $150K when the org has $30K in revenue |
| **Expense reimbursement with receipts** | ✅ Yes | ❌ Reimbursing yourself with no receipts for "miscellaneous expenses" |
| **Board-approved home office stipend** | ✅ Yes | ❌ Paying yourself rent for a home office at above-market rates |
| **Health insurance as part of compensation** | ✅ Yes | ❌ Having the org buy you a car for personal use |

### The safe harbor: Comparability data

**Before setting any founder compensation**, get comparability data:
- What do EDs of similar-sized MA maternal health nonprofits earn?
- What does Colette's hourly rate at Wheaton suggest for consulting?
- What would a bookkeeper or operations person in Greater Boston cost?

Document this comparability data in the board minutes. This is your best defense in an IRS audit.

---

## 3.2 Excess Benefit Transactions

| Rule | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Section 4958** | If a disqualified person (founder, board member, key officer) receives an excess benefit from the org, both the person and the managers who approved it face penalties |
| **Excess benefit** | Any economic benefit that exceeds the value of the goods or services provided in return |
| **Penalty** | 25% excise tax on the excess benefit + 200% tax if not corrected |

### What this means practically

| Scenario | Safe? |
|---|---|
| The org pays Colette $40K/year for 8 hrs/week of ED work | ✅ Safe — reasonable rate, documented, board-approved by disinterested directors |
| The org pays Colette $150K/year for the same 8 hrs/week | ❌ Excess benefit — the value exceeds what the org would pay an unrelated person |
| The org buys a car that Colette uses 90% for personal errands | ❌ Excess benefit |
| The org hires Zack as a paid consultant for bookkeeping at market rate | ✅ Safe |

| **Health insurance** | Employment contract + board resolution |
| **Retirement contributions** | Employment contract + plan documents |
| **Cell phone / internet** | Board resolution with % usage methodology |
