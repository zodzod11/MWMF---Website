# ZD + RG Meeting: Talking Points & Script

**Your Position Going In**

| Fact | Leverage |
|---|---|
| No upfront fee demanded | Good — you're not paying to talk |
| He wants access to your world | You have capabilities and a network he finds valuable |
| 68 Smith Road is a real opportunity | He's already invested time and wants to work with you |
| His draft is one-directional | You can see it clearly — he should know you're not naive |

You walk in with **no money at stake** and **something he wants**. That's a strong position.

---

**Your Objectives (In Order)**

1. **Establish mutual commission** — you get paid for deals you bring him
2. **Narrow the scope** — deal-by-deal, not a blanket agreement
3. **Cap the fee** — protect yourself from an uncapped 3% on a huge deal
4. **Kill the indefinite tail** — no fees after the agreement ends (or 90 days max)
5. **Keep the term short** — 6 months, no auto-renewal

---

## Script

### Opening (Set the tone)

> *"Paris, I appreciate you sending the Scope of Services. I read it carefully, and I like the direction. I think there's real potential here. I have some thoughts on how to make it work better for both of us."*

**Why this works:** You're positive first. You're not attacking or rejecting. You're *improving.*

---

### Point 1 — Mutual Commission (The Main Event)

> *"The way you've structured this, you get paid when you bring me a deal. That makes sense. But I want to make sure it goes both ways. I have my own network, my own relationships. If I bring a deal to you or someone in your network, I should get the same 3% you'd get from me. That's a real partnership."*

**What he might say:**

| Pushback | Your response |
|---|---|
| "That's not how we typically do it" | *"I understand. But you called this a partnership. A one-way partnership isn't a partnership. If it's truly mutual, that should be reflected in the terms."* |
| "What kind of deals could you bring?" | *"You don't know my network yet. Neither of us knows what opportunities could come from either side. That's exactly why the terms should be the same — so we're both incentivized to bring everything to the table."* |
| "We can talk about that later" | *"Let's agree on the principle now. We can work out the details as we go. But the principle needs to be there."* |

---

### Point 2 — Deal-by-Deal Scope

> *"I don't want to sign a blanket agreement where everything I do falls under a 3% obligation. I'd rather we agree on specific opportunities in writing before we pursue them. That keeps it clean — we both know exactly what's covered and what's not."*

**Why:** This is your most important guardrail. Without it, any deal you do during the term could be claimed as "indirectly" related to RG.

---

### Point 3 — Fee Cap

> *"Three percent is fair, but I want a cap on it. I'm thinking $10,000 per opportunity. That way you're still incentivized on big deals, and I'm protected from an uncapped fee on something that scales beyond what either of us expected."*

**What he might say:**

| Pushback | Your response |
|---|---|
| "Why cap it if we're both benefiting?" | *"Because a $10M deal shouldn't cost me $300K for an introduction. The work doesn't scale linearly with deal size. A cap keeps it reasonable."* |
| "That limits our upside" | *"The cap limits the fee, not the deal. You're still getting paid well — $10K for an introduction is excellent compensation."* |

---

### Point 4 — Sunset / Tail

> *"One thing I need to change: your draft says the 3% survives even after the agreement ends. That doesn't work for me. If this relationship ends, I don't want to owe fees years later for something that traces back to this period. I'd propose a 90-day tail — if a deal closes within 90 days of an introduction made during our term, the fee applies. After that, it's clean."*

---

### Point 5 — Term

> *"You proposed one year with auto-renewal. I'd rather do six months to start. We're both testing this relationship. Six months gives us enough time to know if it works. If it does, we renew by mutual agreement."*

---

### Closing

> *"I'm excited about working together. 68 Smith Road is real, and I think there's more where that came from. I just want to make sure the foundation is fair. If we can agree on these points, I'm ready to sign and get moving."*

---

## Cheat Sheet (Key Phrases)

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| He resists mutual commission | *"A one-way partnership isn't a partnership."* |
| He says the terms are standard | *"I'm not looking for standard. I'm looking for fair."* |
| He asks what you bring | *"You'll find out when we start working together. That's why the terms should be mutual."* |
| He wants a long term | *"Let's prove it works first. Six months, then renew."* |
| He won't budge on a point | *"I'm not trying to kill the deal. I'm trying to make it work for both of us. Where's the middle ground?"* |

---

## Bottom Line

| If he agrees to your key points | If he fights you on the basics |
|---|---|
| Shake his hand. Say you'll send the revised Scope of Services reflecting what you discussed. | You've learned everything you need to know. He wants a pipeline, not a partner. Walk away. |

**You hold the cards here.** He wants access to your world. Don't give it to him for free.
