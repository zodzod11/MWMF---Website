# 1. Brand Identity & Visual Direction

## Tone

| Audience | Tone |
|---|---|
| **Mothers & families** | Warm, nonjudgmental, strength-based, culturally affirming. Speaks *with* them, not *at* them. Uses plain language. Avoids clinical jargon. |
| **Donors & grantors** | Professional, evidence-based, mission-driven. Grounded in Colette's research. Clearly states the problem, the solution, and the measurable impact. |
| **Partners (churches, clinics, hospitals)** | Collaborative, credible, professional. Positions the org as a trusted bridge. |

## Color Palette (Proposed)

| Color | Role | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep Teal** `#0A4D5C` | Primary brand color | Nurturing, trustworthy, professional. Not "pink for girls" — avoids the stereotype that maternal health = soft/pastel. Teal is also the color of ovarian cancer awareness and trauma-informed care movements. |
| **Warm Gold/Amber** `#D4A634` | Accent | Warmth, hope, cultural richness. Evokes sun, Caribbean light, Haitian flag gold. |
| **Cream/Warm White** `#FFF8F0` | Background | Soft, welcoming, reduces clinical feel |
| **Deep Charcoal** `#2C2C2C` | Text | High contrast, readable |
| **Terracotta/Clay** `#C46B4F` | Secondary accent | Earth, grounding, connection to community, resilience |

## Typography

| Use | Suggestion |
|---|---|
| **Headings** | Serif or slab serif (warm, grounded) — e.g., **EB Garamond**, **Lora**, or **Source Serif** |
| **Body text** | Clean sans-serif for readability — e.g., **Inter**, **Nunito Sans**, **Source Sans** |
| **Haitian Creole materials** | Ensure typeface supports accented characters (è, ò, à, etc.) |

## Imagery Style

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| ✅ Real photos of Black mothers, families, communities (authentic, diverse skin tones) | ❌ Stock photography of white women touching their bellies |
| ✅ Warm, natural light, real settings (homes, community centers, churches) | ❌ Sterile clinical environments |
| ✅ Intergenerational (mother + baby + grandmother) | ❌ Isolated mothers looking sad |
| ✅ Strength and connection — mothers laughing, being held, being supported | ❌ Images that pathologize or pity mothers with PPD |

**Image sources:** Hire a local photographer for a half-day shoot when you have programming running. In the meantime, use **Nappy** (free diverse stock photography, nappy.co) or **Pexels** with diverse keywords.

## Logo Direction

| Element | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Symbol** | Something that evokes connection, reconnection, or a bridge. Two overlapping circles? A knot? A spiral (mother → baby → family → community)? |
| **Text** | "Women Maternal Wellness Foundation" — clean, readable at small sizes (will appear on mobile, social media, grant PDFs) |
| **Color** | Deep teal primary, gold accent |
| **Subtext** | "Culturally Responsive Care for Every Mother" or "Greater Boston" |

**Don't overbuild the logo now.** A clean wordmark + one symbol concept is enough for Year 1. You can invest in a full identity system when you have funding.

## About Page

### Our Story

Tells Colette's origin story:
- Haitian American nurse and researcher
- PhD in Nursing from UMass Medical School
- Saw Haitian immigrant women falling through the cracks postpartum
- Lived experience, community experience, research experience — all pointed to one gap: culturally responsive postpartum support
- "The research was clear. The need was urgent. The organization was born."

### Core Framework: CONNECT → NAVIGATE → RECONNECT

| Stage | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **CONNECT** | Peer support circles, family education, trusted relationships — so no mother feels alone |
| **NAVIGATE** | Practical help — appointments, insurance, referrals, transportation, childcare — because knowing where to go isn't enough |
| **RECONNECT** | Mothers reconnect with themselves, their families, their communities, their sense of well-being |

### Research Foundation
- Li Fem Anpil dissertation (2018) — lived experience of Haitian immigrant women with PPD
- Boston Black Birthing People Study (2021–2023) — 121 Black birthing people, 61% postpartum follow-up, 34% missed
- Key stat to feature: *"61% of Black birthing people in Greater Boston attended postpartum follow-up. 34% missed — not because they didn't care, but because of systemic barriers: COVID fears, work conflicts, childcare, transportation."*

### Team (placeholder until you have photos)
- Dr. Colette Dieujuste, Founder & Executive Director
- Board of Directors (list names if public, or "Our Board")

## For Mothers & Families Page

This is the most important page on the site. It's the one mothers will find at 2 AM when they're searching for help.

**Tone:** Warm, destigmatizing, direct. No clinical language.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Headline** | *"Are you struggling after childbirth? You are not alone. You are not broken."* |
| **You might be experiencing PPD if...** | Bullet list of symptoms — not clinical, plain language: "Feeling numb or disconnected from your baby," "Can't sleep even when the baby sleeps," "Feeling like you're failing," "Withdrawing from friends and family" |
| **Why it happens** | *"Postpartum depression is not a sign of weakness. It is a medical condition. It is treatable. And it is not your fault."* |
| **You deserve support** | *"You deserve to feel connected to yourself, your baby, and your community. You deserve care that understands your culture, your language, your experience."* |
| **How to reach us** | Warm, low-barrier contact form. No long intake forms. Just: name, best way to reach you, how can we help. |
| **In crisis?** | Crisis hotline numbers prominently displayed (988, Postpartum Support International helpline: 1-800-944-4774) |

# 4. Design & UX Recommendations

## Navigation

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Sticky header** | Logo + nav menu stays at top as user scrolls. Mobile hamburger menu below ~768px. |
| **Language toggle** | Eventually: English / Haitian Creole. Year 1: English only, with a note on Contact page that you serve Haitian Creole speakers. |
| **CTA button in header** | "Get Support" — always visible. Not hidden in a menu. |

## Mobile-First

| Stat | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| 70%+ of your audience will visit on a phone | Mothers searching for help at 2 AM are on their phone, not a laptop. Design for mobile first. |

## Accessibility

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| **WCAG 2.1 AA compliance** | Legal standard. Also: some of your audience may have low vision, low literacy, or be reading in a second language. |
| **Readable font size** | 16px minimum for body text |
| **High color contrast** | Deep charcoal on cream — not light gray on white |
| **Alt text on all images** | Screen reader accessible |
| **Simple language** | Plain English at 6th–8th grade reading level for mother-facing content |

# 6. Technical Implementation Path

Following the digital presence plan: **Carrd first, upgrade later.**

## Phase 1 — Carrd MVP ($19/year)

| Capability | Carrd Can Do | Carrd Can't |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ One-page site with sections | ✅ | ❌ Multiple pages |
| ✅ Custom domain (`womenmaternalwellness.org`) | ✅ | ❌ Blog |
| ✅ Contact form (embed Google Form or JotForm) | ✅ | ❌ Complex navigation |
| ✅ Donation button (embed PayPal Giving Fund) | ✅ | ❌ Event calendar |
| ✅ Clean, professional design | ✅ | ❌ Content management system |
| ✅ Mobile-responsive | ✅ | |

### Carrd Structure for Phase 1

One page with scrollable sections:

```
[Hero] – "Every mother deserves to feel connected..."
    ↓
[Our Mission] – 2-sentence mission statement
    ↓
[The Problem] – PPD disparity, Colette's research, 1–2 stats
    ↓
[Our Approach] – CONNECT → NAVIGATE → RECONNECT visual
    ↓
[Our Programs] – 3 cards (Support Circles, Education, Navigation)
    ↓
[Get Support] – Contact form for mothers
    ↓
[Get Involved] – Volunteer / Partner / Donate buttons
    ↓
[About Dr. Colette] – Brief bio + credentials
    ↓
[Contact] – Email, social, crisis hotlines
    ↓
[Footer] – Copyright, 501(c)(3) status, tax ID
```

**Time to build:** One evening.

## Phase 2 — Squarespace Upgrade ($16/mo)

| Trigger to upgrade | What you gain |
|---|---|
| You need multiple pages | Full site structure (About, Programs, For Mothers, etc.) |
| You need a blog | Built-in blog with categories, tags, RSS |
| You need an event calendar | Squarespace has an events block |
| You need a more polished look | Better templates, more customization |
| You're applying for grants and the site is part of the impression | SEO, analytics, professional appearance |

### Squarespace Template Recommendations

| Template | Why |
|---|---|
| **Tremont** | Clean, content-forward. Good for nonprofits. |
| **Mojave** | Storytelling-focused. Good for the "Our Story" narrative. |
| **Bedford** | Classic nonprofit layout. Hero image + programs grid. |

## Phase 3 — WordPress (If Needed)

| Trigger to upgrade | What you gain |
|---|---|
| You have staff who need to edit the site independently | Full CMS |
| You want complex functionality | Event registration, donor portal, volunteer management |
| You want full control over SEO, performance, analytics | Self-hosted, unlimited customization |

**Don't start here.** WordPress is powerful but requires ongoing maintenance (updates, security, backups) that you don't have time for in Year 1.
