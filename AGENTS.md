## 1. Organization Identity

### Name
- **[DECIDED — Oct 8, 2026]** **Women Maternal Wellness Foundation, Inc.**
- The legal nonprofit name filed with MA Secretary of the Commonwealth and IRS
- **Li Fem Anpil** is the title of Colette's dissertation — not the org name; possible program name for Haitian-specific initiatives
- **"Advancing Postpartum Equity"** is a research presentation title
- **"Connect → Navigate → Reconnect"** is the operational framework/model
- **Domain:** womenmaternalwellness.org (recommended)

### Mission (Colette's written version)
> Our mission is to improve postpartum mental and physical health and advance birth equity for Black, Haitian, immigrant, and other underserved mothers and families in Greater Boston. Through culturally congruent education, early identification of postpartum depression, family and peer support, spiritual and community partnerships, care navigation, and advocacy, we help mothers feel connected, understood, and empowered to obtain the care they need throughout the postpartum year.

### Vision (Colette's written version)
> We envision communities where every mother, regardless of race, culture, language, income, or immigration status, feels heard, respected, connected, and supported after childbirth. We seek a future where postpartum depression is recognized without shame, culturally responsive help is readily available, families and communities know how to support mothers, and preventable maternal suffering and death are eliminated.

### Core concept
- **[DECIDED]** **Disconnection → Reconnection** — rooted in Colette's research
- **[WORKING]** Operationalized as: **CONNECT → NAVIGATE → RECONNECT**
- The nonprofit is a culturally trusted bridge, not a standalone clinic.

### Branding / tone
- **[WORKING]** Culturally responsive, trustworthy, community-based, compassionate/professional, nonjudgmental, strength-based, shame-reducing
- **[WORKING]** Culturally Haitian-aware without excluding other Black mothers
- **[WORKING]** Spirituality available when desired, not imposed
- **[OPEN]** No final logo, colors, visual identity, tagline, or official values statement

## 3. Services & Programs

### Core philosophy
- **[DECIDED]** Function as a **trusted bridge**, not a standalone mental-health clinic
- Nonprofit: identifies needs, educates, screens, provides peer/community support, navigates, makes warm referrals, follows up, reduces practical barriers
- Actual clinical treatment comes from licensed partner providers

### Program 1 — Maternal Wellness and Support Circles
- **[WORKING]** Culturally responsive, confidential peer groups in English and Haitian Creole
- Peer support + professional guidance + spirituality when desired
- Year 1 concept: ~1 group/month

### Program 2 — Fourth Trimester Education and Family Connection Program
- **[WORKING]** Community workshops through churches, clinics, hospitals, community orgs
- Topics: PPD, warning signs, healthy communication, partner/family support, where to seek help
- Intentionally involves the family

### Program 3 — Maternal Wellness Navigation and Home-Support Program
- **[WORKING]** Viewed as the backbone of the organization
- Personnel: nurse, social worker, doula, CHW, navigator
- Services: screening, warm referrals, appointment scheduling, insurance assistance, home visits, telehealth, childcare/transportation referrals, follow-up

### What the org will NOT do initially
- **[DECIDED]** Not a full psychiatric/therapy clinic at launch
- Not duplicating Postpartum Support International or MA state programs
- Not forcing religion/spirituality
- Not depending solely on mothers' resilience

### Pricing
- **[DECIDED]** No client fees. Grant/philanthropy-supported.

## 8. Research Evidence Base

### Li Fem Anpil (Colette's PhD)
- **Theme 1: Feeling Disconnected** — lack of support, partner conflict, nostalgia/separation from Haiti
- **Theme 2: Feeling Reconnected** — realizing help was needed, spirituality, resilience

### Cultural barriers
- Mental-health stigma, fear of being judged/labeled/inadequate
- Separation from home-country support networks
- Immigration/Haiti-related stress
- Partner conflict and poor social support

### Boston Black Birthing People Study
- 121 Black birthing people, Greater Boston
- Postpartum follow-up: 61% attended, 34% missed
- Barriers: COVID fears 39%, work constraints 34%, childcare 27%
- **[DECIDED CONCLUSION]** Systemic inequities, not individual failure
