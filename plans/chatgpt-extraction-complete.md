## 8. Research & Evidence Base

### Li Fem Anpil — Haitian immigrant PPD study (Colette's PhD)
- Two aims: (1) lived experience of PPD among Haitian immigrant women, (2) how being Haitian influenced responses
- **Theme 1: Feeling Disconnected** — lack of support, partner conflict, nostalgia/separation from Haiti
- **Theme 2: Feeling Reconnected** — realizing help was needed, spirituality, resilience

### Major cultural barriers identified
- Mental-health stigma (weakness, private family matter, fear of being judged/labeled)
- Fear of being considered an inadequate mother
- Separation from home-country support networks
- Immigration/Haiti-related stress (violence, instability, acculturation, language/cultural differences)
- Partner conflict and poor social support

### Boston Black Birthing People Study (Colette's later work)
- 121 Black birthing people, Greater Boston, delivered within 24 months
- Postpartum follow-up: 61% attended, 34% missed, 5% declined
- College degree + attendance: 77% (college) vs 46% (less than college)
- Private insurance + attendance: 77% vs 55% public insurance
- Logistical barriers: COVID fears 39%, work constraints 34%, childcare 27%
- Central conclusion: systemic and gendered racial inequities shape outcomes

### Interventions highlighted by research
- Cultural humility, prenatal/postpartum planning, symptom education, telehealth
- Postpartum home visiting, family/medical leave, doula access
- Community advocacy, improved Medicaid/postpartum support
